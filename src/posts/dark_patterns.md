**Dark Patterns** _(also known as Deceptive Design Patterns)_

User interfaces carefully crafted to trick users into doing things that benefit the designer — from paying more than necessary, clicking unintended actions, or struggling to find options that cost the designer money. [deceptive.design](https://deceptive.design/) is one of the most well-known sites that tracks and documents these patterns.

---

**The Most Common Patterns**

1. **Bait and Switch** — Mostly executed through misleading advertising: a company promises something, or over-promises, to grab your attention. Once you've signed up or subscribed, the fine print emerges — what was advertised as free has hidden costs, or you don't actually "qualify" for the benefits that were portrayed as freely available.
    
2. **Drip Pricing** - Common in online shopping. The advertised price isn't the final price. You go through the whole process — picking colors, sizes, options — only to see the true total at checkout. By then you've already committed mentally and invested your time, so you pay it anyway.
    
3. **Confirm Shaming** - The interface uses guilt-inducing wording to discourage certain choices. The classic example: _"No thanks, I don't want to save money."_ Simple, but effective.
    
4. **Misdirection** - The interface is designed to mislead. An acceptance button may be styled or worded to look like a decline, sometimes using double negatives such as _"Don't not sell my information."_ Another example: mid-download prompts that present a separate, unwanted download as though it's a requirement for what you actually wanted.
    
5. **Roach Motel** - Signing up is frictionless and easy. Cancelling, however, is deliberately long and tedious — so users either give up or keep putting it off.

6. **Fake Urgency** - This one usually comes as fake count downs, as in you need to quickly do something before it runs out, mostly used to make you click into ads or make a purchase under duress.
   
7. **Forced Continuity** - free Trials require you to input your bank details and then when the trial ends the are no reminders they just start charging with hopes that you won't notice

### How to easily spot Dark patterns
- First you need keep an eye for any of the above mention tricks.
- Read carefully on everything you a clicking especially during checkout or final stages of any online process this is the time the employed tricks usually show up
- Make sure you do things with intention and slow down, making sure that you meet your final result still align with your initial goal.
